ADHD-friendly planner re-entry and overwhelm

key findings
Adult-ADHD CBT treats a calendar plus task-list system as a core compensatory skill, explicitly separating appointments/schedules from to-do items while keeping them in one trusted system.
Planning support should externalize memory and decisions: capture everything once, distinguish fixed commitments from flexible work, and translate vague tasks into small observable next actions.
A complete overview and a focused action view serve different needs. The planner should preserve a single-glance weekly/monthly landscape but progressively reveal detail and offer a very small “Now / Next” subset to prevent the overview itself becoming overstimulating.
Adults with ADHD can be more susceptible to visual interference and can take longer to disengage from distractors. Calm hierarchy, restrained color, spacing, filters, and optional focus mode are functional accessibility choices—not cosmetic minimalism.
NICE describes useful environmental modifications including reduced distractions, written reinforcement, shorter focus periods, and movement breaks. Planner implications include explicit break buffers, realistic capacity, and visible written cues.
For re-entry after travel or disruption, avoid treating backlog as today’s plan. Use a reset flow: collect loose ends, triage into must/should/could/later, identify deadlines and fixed events, archive or defer without guilt, then choose a tiny restart set.
Implementation-intention and prospective-memory research supports connecting an intention to a concrete cue or time. A planner can capture “when/where/after what” and optionally prompt the user to imagine the first action, especially for important time-bound tasks.
Motivation support should emphasize initiation and comeback rather than perfect streaks: celebrate starting, partial progress, re-planning, and returning after absence; retain Bingo/checklist feedback without turning accumulated incompletion into a verdict.

open product questions
How much information remains legible in week versus month view? Which items deserve interruption-style reminders? Should “appointment/meeting” be one fixed-commitment type or separate types? What is the owner’s preferred maximum daily restart set—one anchor plus two flexible items, or another capacity rule?
owner context
Recent travel interrupted the planning rhythm. The immediate need is not a larger backlog display; it is a trustworthy map of obligations plus a gentle re-entry mechanism that reveals what exists, identifies what is truly compulsory, and makes the first move feel small and obvious.
